# Narrative Structure & Flow

Read this before deciding *what* goes on any slide. A presentation is a time-based medium; the narrative arc is more important than individual slide design.

For AI/database products, the narrative arc must be built from claims and evidence, not from slide templates. Each major claim should have a visible support path: metric, query result, chart, screenshot, source note, customer quote, or explicit assumption.

## The "Audience Decision Flow"

Do not just list features or facts. Structure the presentation around the questions the audience is implicitly asking.

### For Pitch Decks (VC / Seed)
Answer these questions in this order:
1. **The Hook:** Why should I care right now? (The massive shift or undeniable truth).
2. **The Problem:** What is the specific, painful problem? (Make it visceral).
3. **The Solution:** How do you solve it uniquely? (Show, don't explain).
4. **The "Why Now?":** Why hasn't this been done before? (Tech shift, market timing).
5. **Traction/Validation:** Does anyone care? (Revenue, active users, pilots).
6. **Market Size:** Is this big enough to matter? (TAM, but bottom-up is better).
7. **Business Model:** How do we make money?
8. **The Team:** Why are you the only people who can do this?
9. **The Ask:** What do you need, and what milestone will it buy?

### For Board Updates / Executive Summaries
1. **The TL;DR:** What is the one thing I need to know? (e.g., "Q3 was strong, but churn is a risk").
2. **Highlights:** What went right?
3. **Lowlights/Risks:** What went wrong and how are we fixing it?
4. **Key Metrics:** The 3-5 numbers that matter.
5. **Strategic Asks:** Decisions needed from the board/executives.

### For Data / Research Decks
1. **The Question:** What decision or uncertainty triggered the analysis?
2. **The Short Answer:** What did the data show?
3. **The Evidence:** Which 3-5 findings prove the answer?
4. **The Nuance:** What changed by segment, time period, cohort, region, customer type, or scenario?
5. **The Risk:** What assumptions, missing data, or confidence limits matter?
6. **The Action:** What should the audience do next?

### For Product Demos / Sales
1. **The Current State:** The painful, expensive, or slow way things are done today.
2. **The Vision:** The promised land.
3. **The Product:** The "Aha!" moment (Core feature that bridges the gap).
4. **The Proof:** Case studies or ROI data.
5. **Next Steps:** Clear CTA.

## Pacing Rules

- **The 3-Minute Rule:** If it takes more than 3 minutes to explain a slide, break it into 3 slides.
- **The "Punchline First" Rule:** The title of the slide should be the conclusion.
  - *Bad:* "Q3 Financial Performance"
  - *Good:* "Q3 Revenue Doubled Driven by Enterprise Sales"
- **Build Tension and Release:** Alternate between high-density data slides and clean, single-statement "breather" slides.
- **Evidence Before Polish:** A visually strong slide with an unsupported claim is a broken slide.
