# Design Principles

## Intent Before Decoration

Every page needs a clear job. Before styling, identify what the user should understand, decide, or do. A visually rich page still fails if hierarchy, task flow, or content priority is unclear.

## One Coherent Visual Idea

Strong pages are not collections of nice effects. They have a dominant visual idea:

- Precision and restraint.
- Warm craft and product focus.
- Playful productivity.
- Dark command-center utility.
- Editorial clarity.
- Expressive collaboration.

Choose one primary direction, then let tokens, layout, imagery, and motion support it.

## Hierarchy Through Contrast

Use contrast intentionally:

- Size contrast for message priority.
- Weight contrast for labels and actions.
- Surface contrast for grouping.
- Color contrast for action and state.
- Spatial contrast for section rhythm.

Avoid relying on color alone to communicate priority or state.

## Designed Restraint

Restraint is active design, not absence of design. Use fewer accents, shadows, gradients, or shapes when the product needs credibility, focus, or premium feel. Use expressive elements when they clarify mood, category, or action.

## Systematic Variation

A page should feel varied without feeling random. Variation should come from a small system:

- Two or three surface levels.
- A limited color palette with optional accents.
- A predictable radius scale.
- A consistent spacing unit.
- A type scale with defined roles.

## Real Interface States

High-quality web UI includes states, not just static blocks:

- Hover.
- Focus-visible.
- Active.
- Disabled.
- Loading.
- Error.
- Empty.
- Selected.

States should feel native to the visual system.

## Content Density Is A Design Choice

Comfortable pages use larger gaps, bigger section rhythm, and more negative space. Dense pages use tighter spacing, smaller type, stronger grouping, and clearer dividers. Pick density based on task, not taste.

## Atmosphere Without Noise

Atmosphere can come from color temperature, gradients, surface layering, typography, imagery, or motion. Avoid decorative elements that do not support hierarchy, brand feel, or comprehension.

## Implementation Is Part Of Design

Good design must survive in code:

- Tokens should be named and reusable.
- Layout should be responsive.
- Components should have states.
- CSS should not become one-off decoration.
- Accessibility cannot be patched on at the end.
